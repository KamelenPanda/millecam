## Priority Issues (found and fixed this pass)

**[P1] Diensten-teaser showed all 5 services, not a teaser.** The homepage mapped every `DIENSTEN` item into a `md:grid-cols-2` grid, orphaning the 5th card alone in its row and undercutting the point of the adjacent "Bekijk alle diensten" link (nothing new to reveal). Exceeded the page's own ≤4-items-per-group rhythm.
**Fix applied**: homepage teaser now shows `DIENSTEN.slice(0, 4)` (nl/en/fr); `/diensten` (and its EN/FR equivalents) still list the full catalog of 5.

**[P2] Locale-switcher active-language label failed WCAG AA by a hair.** `text-terracotta` (#B2532E) on paper background measured 4.31:1 at 12px — just under the 4.5:1 floor. Not caught by the prior pass because it's a different element than what was fixed then.
**Fix applied**: swapped to `text-terracotta-darker` (#8F3F20 → 6.2:1 on paper), a real margin instead of another razor-thin pass.

**[P2] Cookie-consent banner overlapped page content across most of the scroll range.** Confirmed mechanically at both 1440×900 and 390×844: being `position: fixed` and undismissed-by-default, it sat on top of section headings, body copy, and — at one scroll position — actual footer links (LinkedIn, Cookiebeleid, NIS2-check), for as long as the visitor scrolled without clicking "Begrepen." This is the same banner an earlier session pass had already tried to reposition (right vs. left corner) without addressing the actual cause: a fixed element doesn't stop covering content just because it moves corners.
**Fix applied**: the banner now hides itself once the visitor scrolls past ~400px (and reappears if they scroll back up near the top) — it's a notice, not a consent gate, so nothing is lost by no longer chasing the visitor down the page. `localStorage`-based permanent dismissal via "Begrepen" is unchanged.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer)**: Hits four unexplained acronyms in the hero before any plain-language help; the one tool built to answer "does this even apply to me?" (NIS2-check) is two sections down rather than beside the framework badges.

**Riley (stress-tester)**: Clicking "Erkend Data Protection Officer" leads to the general `/over` page, not a credentials/verification proof point — a minor letdown exactly where a skeptical reader is testing the claim.

**Casey (distracted mobile user)**: The self-assessment's rating buttons are ~14px wide with small gaps — fiddly under a thumb, turning a delight feature into friction on the device most visitors likely arrive on first.

## Minor Observations

- Contrast margins on some brand tokens are thin by design intent, not accident (e.g. primary button ≈4.65:1) — worth a documented minimum rather than relying on memory that "we fixed this once."
- The bookend CTA repeats verbatim at hero and close; a small copy variation at the close (acknowledging the reader has now seen the case) would land closer to true peak-end design than a literal repeat.
- Hero ships two custom-styled trust-badge pills outside the `Button` component's own "one primary CTA" discipline — not wrong, but worth naming since the rest of the codebase enforces that rule deliberately.

## Questions to Consider

- Is the interactive scoring widget the best use of hero space for a visitor who doesn't yet know whether NIS2/ISO27001 even applies to them — or should the NIS2-check tool itself lead?
- Would surfacing one plain-language definition inline (a tooltip, or a one-line gloss) next to the framework acronyms resolve the recall gap without disrupting the hero's density?
